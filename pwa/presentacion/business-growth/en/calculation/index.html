Client universe

300 to 500 Chinese companies selling in Mexico

Those that can hire a business-development service.

How it is derived

  1. There are more than 1,400 companies with Chinese capital registered with foreign direct investment in Mexico (Ministry of Economy, 2026); 1,302 between 2006 and September 2025 (AMPIP). Counting intermediaries and representative offices, the Mexico-China Chamber of Commerce and Technology estimates 4,000 to 5,000.
  2. Not all of them sell in Mexico: many are maquila operations exporting to the United States. It is assumed that 30 to 40% of the registered companies sell to the Mexican market (auto parts to local assemblers, machinery, electronics, retail and e-commerce, consumer goods): 1,400 × 0.3 = 420 and 1,400 × 0.4 = 560.
  3. From those, the very small ones with no budget are discounted: a serviceable universe of 300 to 500 remains. It includes the 60 to 80 Chinese consumer brands from the Media Buying study and the B2B companies (auto parts, machinery, energy, electronics).
  4. New entrants are added every year: 42 Chinese projects announced in Mexico in 2024 (ECLAC, 57% of the value announced in the region) and 30 projects expected in 2025. The State of Mexico alone has 80 Chinese companies and 16 about to arrive.

Assumption to validate: there is no public registry of Chinese companies by type of operation in Mexico. The share selling to the local market is corrected with the Mexico-China Chamber's directory. Chinese investment fell 80% in 2025 because of the tariffs and rebounded to a record in the first half of 2026 (US$596 million): the flow of new companies is volatile.

Pilot scenario

$1.8 million MXN in 6 months

Two Chinese companies: one Entry and one Growth.

How it is derived

  1. Implementations: $60,000 + $100,000 = $160,000 MXN.
  2. Monthly fees: ($45,000 + $85,000) × 6 months = $780,000 MXN.
  3. Variables: the first month is setup and produces no meetings; 5 months are charged: ($66,000 + $105,000) × 5 = $855,000 MXN.
  4. Total: $160,000 + $780,000 + $855,000 = $1,795,000, rounded to $1.8 million MXN. That is $300,000 MXN a month on average.

The pilot lasts 6 months because a qualified meeting takes 4 to 8 weeks to materialize and an accepted opportunity 2 to 4 months: with 90 days only meetings would be seen, not opportunities.

Base scenario

$12 million MXN a year

Year 1: 8 clients at year end, 5 on average during the year.

How it is derived

  1. 8 clients are signed during the year (3 Entry, 3 Growth, 2 Expansion); since they come in staggered, the average active count is 5 (2 Entry, 2 Growth, 1 Expansion).
  2. Implementations for the 8: 3 × $60,000 + 3 × $100,000 + 2 × $150,000 = $780,000 MXN.
  3. Monthly fees for the average 5: (2 × $45,000 + 2 × $85,000 + $150,000) × 12 = $410,000 × 12 = $4.92 million MXN.
  4. Variables for the average 5: (2 × $66,000 + 2 × $105,000 + $165,000) × 12 = $507,000 × 12 = $6.08 million MXN.
  5. Total: $0.78 + $4.92 + $6.08 = $11.78 million MXN, rounded to $12 million MXN.
High scenario

$45 million MXN a year

Year 2 or 3: 20 clients active all year.

How it is derived

  1. 20 clients: 8 Entry, 8 Growth and 4 Expansion, active for 12 months; plus 8 new clients that join during the year and pay implementation.
  2. New implementations: 3 × $60,000 + 3 × $100,000 + 2 × $150,000 = $780,000 MXN.
  3. Monthly fees: (8 × $45,000 + 8 × $85,000 + 4 × $150,000) × 12 = $1,640,000 × 12 = $19.68 million MXN.
  4. Variables: (8 × $66,000 + 8 × $105,000 + 4 × $165,000) × 12 = $2,028,000 × 12 = $24.34 million MXN.
  5. Total: $0.78 + $19.68 + $24.34 = $44.8 million MXN, rounded to $45 million MXN. It is a ceiling, not a target.

Split

40% operating cost, 60% margin in three equal parts

What each party receives: Xinhua, Intiviso and whoever makes the sale.

How it is derived

  1. There is no third party to pay (no phone brand, no creator): the revenue is the service fee. The cost of operating it (data, prospecting tools, SDR and analyst staff, CRM, measurement) is absorbed by Intiviso and estimated at 40% of revenue.
  2. The 60% margin is split in three equal parts of 20% each: Xinhua, Intiviso and the party that makes the sale (Xinhua's sales and marketing or a third party). It is the same one-third scheme as MX News and Media Buying.

The pilot and base cases are defensible: they capture less than 3% of the Chinese companies selling in Mexico. The high case requires capturing one in twenty and assumes Xinhua becomes the recognized gateway for new companies under "China Brands Going Global".

Can the client afford it?

  1. B2B companies allocate on average 7.7% of revenue to marketing (Gartner) and 22% of that budget to agencies and external services: about 1.7% of revenue goes to providers like this one.
  2. The Entry package ($1.4 million MXN a year) fits a company with Mexican sales of $80 million MXN or more; Growth ($2.4 million) one with $140 million or more; Expansion ($3.9 million) one with $230 million or more. Chinese consumer brands, carmakers and auto-parts companies comfortably exceed those sales.
  3. Against the alternative: two in-house SDRs cost $30,000 to $60,000 MXN a month plus tools and a 3-month learning curve, with no market intelligence or network. The service is paid mostly for results, which an employee does not offer.
