Market

$80 to $250 million MXN a year

What Chinese brands spend on content creators in Mexico.

How it is derived

  1. Digital advertising in Mexico reached $124,625 million MXN in 2025, 66.6% of a $191,315 million total (Valor Total Media 2026: AVE, CIM, IAB Mexico).
  2. Influencer marketing in Mexico is worth US$240–300 million a year, about $4,300–5,500 million MXN (Capgemini 2023; Goldfish 2024): 3.5–4.4% of digital spend.
  3. Chinese brands invest $1,900–3,100 million MXN a year in digital advertising in Mexico (MX News calculation detail).
  4. If they allocate 4–8% of that digital spend to creators (above average, because they are new consumer brands that need a local voice): $1,900 × 4% = $76 million and $3,100 × 8% = $248 million. Rounded to $80–250 million MXN a year.
  5. Universe: 60 to 80 Chinese consumer brands in Mexico (cars 20–27, phones 8, TV and home 8–10, e-commerce 6–8, food 2–4, apps 3) plus 30 new Asian brands looking for retail space (Colliers).

Assumption to validate: nobody measures creator spend in Mexico yet (AVE, CIM and IAB Mexico will publish a first figure in 2027) and there is no public figure per Chinese brand. It is corrected with the first 3 real quotes.

Pilot scenario

$2.5 million MXN in 6 months

Three Chinese brands: 1 Presence and 2 Voice, plus 2 campaigns and licenses.

How it is derived

  1. Packages: ($35,000 + 2 × $130,000) × 6 months = $295,000 × 6 = $1.77 million MXN.
  2. Campaigns: 2 × $250,000 = $0.5 million MXN.
  3. Content licenses: 10% on packages and campaigns = $0.23 million MXN.
  4. Total: $1.77 + $0.5 + $0.23 = $2.5 million MXN. That is $417,000 MXN a month on average.
Base scenario

$14 million MXN a year

Year 1: 10 brands at year end, 7 active on average (3 Presence, 5 Voice and 2 Embassy at 70% occupancy).

How it is derived

  1. Packages: (3 × $35,000 + 5 × $130,000 + 2 × $220,000) × 12 × 70% = $1,195,000 × 12 × 0.7 = $10.0 million MXN.
  2. Campaigns: 8 × $250,000 = $2.0 million MXN.
  3. Exclusive ambassador: 1 × $130,000 × 6 months = $0.8 million MXN.
  4. Licenses: 10% on packages and campaigns = $1.2 million MXN.
  5. Total: $10.0 + $2.0 + $0.8 + $1.2 = $14.0 million MXN, i.e. $14 million MXN.
High scenario

$40 million MXN a year

Year 2 or 3: 20 brands active all year (6 Presence, 10 Voice and 4 Embassy).

How it is derived

  1. Packages: (6 × $35,000 + 10 × $130,000 + 4 × $220,000) × 12 = $2,390,000 × 12 = $28.7 million MXN.
  2. Campaigns: 16 × $250,000 = $4.0 million MXN.
  3. Exclusive ambassadors: 2 × $130,000 × 12 = $3.1 million MXN.
  4. Licenses: 10% on packages and campaigns = $3.3 million MXN.
  5. Total: $28.7 + $4.0 + $3.1 + $3.3 = $39.1 million MXN, rounded to $40 million MXN. It is a ceiling, not a target.

Split

Creator ~60%, margin ~40% in three equal parts

What each party receives: Xinhua, Intiviso and whoever makes the sale.

How it is derived

  1. The creator receives about 60% of the price (see packages). The remaining 40% is the margin that pays for production, management, measurement and sales.
  2. That margin is split in three equal parts: Xinhua, Intiviso and the party that makes the sale. It is the same one-third scheme as MX News and Business Growth.

The pilot and base cases are defensible. The high case is ambitious: it requires displacing the agencies of Shein, Temu, Xiaomi and the carmakers, or capturing the 30 new brands arriving.

Risks

  • The directory has a single technology creator; cars and phones require niche creators, which cost more.
  • Credibility: paid content labeled as advertising, never mixed with Xinhua news (propaganda perception).
  • Licenses and exclusivity require contracts with each creator.
